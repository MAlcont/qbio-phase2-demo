How the intended governance flow works
1 · Identity admission

The DAO maintains an offline list of people accepted through a live interview, an existing trusted relationship, or another reliable route. An institutional address—such as a university mailbox—can be a secondary route.

2 · Account registration

The DAO sends an invitation to register. The account binds a 2FA device and may bind one or more Ethereum addresses. A wallet is needed when QBIO holdings are meant to contribute voting weight.

3 · Proposal pipeline

A proposal matures through community discussion and the simplified Phase 1 process, then enters this soft Phase 2 on the DAO site. Later stages may remain manual or be delegated to the DAO account.

4 · Forum and intentions

Eligible voting power is dormant NO by default. An account may change its current intention without publishing an individual on-chain vote. Commenting is separate from changing intention.

  • The backend certifies current account intention; the public interface exposes only the aggregate YES/NO winner, plus aggregate explicit participation after discussion starts.
  • Anonymous or agent accounts may receive reduced effective weight. In this demo the slider makes that reduction visible; at 0%, anonymous power does not affect the winner.
  • The visual uses an approximate 400M QBIO circulating voting-power reference against a 1B QBIO total supply. This reference is currently demo data, not a live on-chain supply query.
  • Placeholder labels: researcher represents the institutional-identification route; member represents an identity-verified account with at least 10,000 QBIO; observer represents an unidentified account with at least 10,000 QBIO.
