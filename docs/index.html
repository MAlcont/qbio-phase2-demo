How the intended governance flow works
1 · Identity admission

The DAO maintains an offline list of people it has accepted through a live interview, an existing trusted relationship, or another reliable route. An institutional address—such as a university mailbox—can be a secondary route to establishing identity.

2 · Account registration

The DAO sends an invitation to register on the site. The account binds a 2FA device and may bind one or more Ethereum addresses. A wallet is needed when QBIO holdings are meant to contribute voting weight; participation in discussion does not require publishing a wallet-address vote.

3 · Proposal pipeline

A proposal matures through community discussion and the simplified Phase 1 process, then enters this soft Phase 2 on the DAO site. Management of the later stages can be delegated to the DAO account; automation is optional.

4 · Forum and intentions

Eligible voting power is dormant NO by default. An account may change its current intention without publishing an individual on-chain vote. Commenting is separate from changing intention. Once discussion exists, the site may disclose the share of total eligible voting power represented by explicit intentions.

  • The backend certifies the current account intention; the public interface exposes only the aggregate YES/NO winner, plus the aggregate explicit-participation percentage after discussion starts.
  • Certified accounts may remain absent/dormant or become explicit. Unidentified accounts or agents may participate with a reduced effective weight; the exact anonymous rebalancing rule is still a demo parameter.
  • When YES is ahead and explicit voting power is high enough that the formal governance step becomes unavoidable, the DAO could advance the proposal to Commons/Snapshot. That transition is not yet automated in this demo.
  • Placeholder labels: researcher represents the institutional-identification route; member represents an identity-verified account with at least 10,000 QBIO; observer represents an unidentified account with at least 10,000 QBIO. Rows explicitly labelled below-threshold exist only to test exclusion from the voting body.
  • The broader aim is to create time and accountability for discussion before formal voting, while giving the DAO a configurable place for proposal analytics and community traffic.
